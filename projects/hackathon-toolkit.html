How might we help students turn scattered academic information into one clear semester plan?

Problem Space

Students were managing academic work across disconnected systems

The core problem was not a lack of productivity tools. Students already had calendars, reminders, LMS portals, syllabi, notes apps, and group chats. The issue was that none of those systems translated course information into a reliable planning workflow. A syllabus might include grading weights, weekly readings, exam windows, attendance policies, office hours, and assignment deadlines, but students still had to manually interpret and re-enter that information into whatever system they actually used.

That manual translation created friction at the exact moment students needed clarity. When deadlines changed, announcements were missed, or grade weights were unclear, students lost trust in their own plans. Mentora was scoped around that gap: making academic information easier to understand, maintain, and act on.

01

Manual Setup

Students spent time copying assignments, quizzes, readings, exams, and project deadlines from syllabi into separate planning tools.

02

Unclear Priorities

When multiple courses competed for attention, students struggled to decide what mattered most each week.

03

Grade Ambiguity

Students often knew a grade percentage but not what future performance would be required to reach their goal.

Student Research

I synthesized 10+ interviews into the product areas students repeated most

Across interviews, the strongest signals were about scattered deadlines, uncertainty around grading policies, calendar friction, and the mental load of constantly checking multiple academic systems. Students did not ask for a broad AI chatbot first. They asked for something that would remove the repetitive setup work and help them understand what to focus on.

Insight 01

Planning Breaks at Setup

The hardest part was not checking a calendar. It was turning raw syllabus information into a calendar students trusted.

Insight 02

Students Need Translation

Dense documents needed to become deadlines, grade rules, study tasks, and class-specific answers.

Insight 03

Trust Matters More Than Magic

For AI to be useful in academics, students needed clear outputs they could review, edit, and rely on.

Users

I designed for overloaded students balancing coursework, jobs, and leadership responsibilities

The target user was a student who wanted to do well but did not have a clean operational system for the semester. They might be taking 12 to 15 credit hours, working part-time, participating in student organizations, and checking multiple class portals each week. The product needed to support motivated students without assuming they had unlimited time or perfect planning habits.

Hania

Busy Planner

A student balancing 15 credit hours and part-time work who needs deadlines, policies, and study plans in one place.

Max

Deadline Reactor

A student who procrastinates because class work feels scattered, then scrambles when deadlines become urgent.

Shared Need

Confidence

Both users needed a planning system that made the next academic step feel visible, realistic, and trustworthy.

Product Strategy

I narrowed the MVP around the workflow that created the most repeated friction

With only 48 hours to build, I treated prioritization as the product decision. The team could not build a full LMS replacement, a social study app, a grade simulator, a calendar platform, and a general-purpose academic chatbot at once. I focused the roadmap on the smallest complete loop: upload a syllabus, extract academic information, organize it into classes and deadlines, then help the student ask course-specific questions through Demi.

Must Have

Syllabus Extraction

Course names, assignments, due dates, grading rules, exam dates, schedules, and policy details had to be pulled from static documents.

Must Have

Planning Output

Extracted data needed to become class cards, calendar events, to-do items, grade data, and weekly study guidance.

Later

Broad AI Features

General tutoring, social study groups, and larger LMS integrations were deferred because they did not solve the first trust problem.

Core Workflow

Drop in a syllabus. Get back a semester plan.

Mentora starts with the source document students already receive: the syllabus. After upload, the system extracts relevant course information and creates synced class cards. From there, the student can open a course, review assignments, add deadlines to a calendar, calculate grade standing, forecast final grade requirements, or ask Demi questions about class-specific details.

01

Extract

Pull course names, assignments, deadlines, grading weights, exam dates, attendance policies, and instructor expectations.

02

Organize

Convert extracted information into course pages, upcoming tasks, calendar events, and grade modules.

03

Advise

Use Demi to answer syllabus-specific questions and help students understand what needs attention next.

Solution Design

I positioned AI as a translation layer, not the entire product

The final product concept included AI-powered syllabus analysis, auto-generated class pages, assignment tracking, calendar integration, personalized study plans, a course-specific Demi assistant, a grade calculator, and final grade forecasting. The key product choice was making AI serve the workflow. Instead of asking students to chat with an empty assistant, Mentora gave them structured outputs first, then let Demi explain or clarify the details.

Grade Tools

Current vs Future

Separated current grade calculation from final grade forecasting because students need both status and direction.

Key Product Decision

Calculation and forecasting solve two different student questions

One important decision was separating grade calculation from final grade forecasting. A calculator answers, "Where am I right now?" A forecast answers, "What do I need next?" Combining those into one feature would have made the product feel simpler on the surface, but less helpful in practice. Keeping them separate made the experience more understandable and better matched to how students think during the semester.

Current Grade

Status

Helps students understand where they stand based on completed work and grading weights.

Final Forecast

Direction

Helps students estimate what they need on future exams, projects, or assignments to reach a target.

PM Tradeoff

Clarity Over Compression

Kept the tools separate so each answered a distinct student need without overloading the interface.

Trust & Risk

The hardest product challenge was making AI feel reliable enough for academic planning

Academic planning has a low tolerance for incorrect information. A hallucinated deadline, wrong exam date, or misread grading policy could cause real stress. Because of that, the experience needed to make AI outputs reviewable instead of invisible. The next version would include source-highlighted extraction, editable deadlines, confidence states, and prompts that tell students when a detail needs manual confirmation.

Risk

Incorrect Extraction

A wrong date or grading rule could damage trust quickly, even if the rest of the system worked well.

Design Response

Editable Outputs

Students should be able to confirm, correct, or dismiss extracted assignments before syncing them.

Future State

Source Evidence

Each generated task should connect back to the exact syllabus text that produced it.

Measurement Plan

I defined success around planning confidence, not just feature usage

Because the product was built quickly, the first measurable outcome was adoption by early users. For a full launch, I would measure whether Mentora reduces the time students spend setting up a semester plan and whether students trust the plan enough to return weekly. The best metric is not simply how many syllabi are uploaded; it is whether the upload creates an ongoing planning habit.

Efficiency

Planning Time Saved

Compare manual syllabus-to-calendar setup time against the Mentora extraction and review flow.

Trust

Edits Per Syllabus

Track how often students need to correct extracted deadlines, policies, or grade weights.

Retention

Weekly Planning Use

Measure whether students come back to check tasks, ask Demi questions, and review grade forecasts.

Next Iteration

The next version would make the system more transparent, connected, and mobile-first

If I continued the project, I would deepen the product around trust and integrations. The strongest next steps would be source-highlighted syllabus extraction, calendar export, LMS/email syncing, smart reminder rules, mobile-first weekly planning, and accessibility improvements for students who rely on screen readers or structured task views.

Reflection

Mentora taught me how to scope AI around a specific user workflow

This project strengthened how I think as a product manager. The most important move was not adding more AI. It was identifying where students lost time and confidence, then designing a system that translated messy academic inputs into useful next steps. I learned to separate what sounds impressive from what actually helps the user, especially under a tight deadline. Mentora worked because the roadmap stayed anchored to one clear promise: make the semester easier to understand.
