The Story

The product opportunity was hiding before the form was submitted

Prior authorization delays often happen after a request reaches the payer, but many preventable issues begin earlier: missing rationale, unclear diagnosis support, incomplete documents, or scattered treatment context. AuthForm focuses on that upstream moment. I designed the product around helping physicians prepare cleaner, insurer-ready requests while keeping clinical review and final judgment in human hands.

How might we reduce preventable ADHD medication approval delays without removing physician control from the workflow?

Workflow Evidence

The dashboard needed to make invisible documentation risk visible

The biggest UX challenge was not displaying more information. It was deciding what information deserved attention before submission. AuthForm organizes ICD-10 codes, treatment requests, clinical rationale, payer-specific needs, and documentation gaps into a scan-friendly review surface.

SPEAKING TO USERS

I treated physicians and care teams as high-context users with very little spare attention

The product could not assume users had time to read a long AI explanation. Physicians need quick confidence: what is missing, why it matters, and what action should happen next. Administrative staff need consistency: the request should be organized enough that handoffs do not lose clinical context.

That pushed the dashboard toward a preparation workflow rather than a generic document generator. Each information block needed a clear job: confirm the patient context, summarize the medication request, surface the risk, and give the physician a final review path.

User Need

Scan Quickly

Use strong labels and short summaries so the review surface can be understood during a busy clinical workflow.

User Need

Trust the Source

Keep important clinical details visible instead of hiding the logic behind an AI-generated paragraph.

User Need

Fix Before Submit

Make documentation gaps visible while there is still time to correct the request.

DEFINING CORE FEATURES

I designed AI as a preparation layer, not an approval machine

Healthcare workflows require trust, accountability, and traceability. AuthForm therefore keeps AI in a support role. It can structure a request, flag missing evidence, and draft payer-ready summaries, but the physician remains the final reviewer. That decision protects clinical context and makes the product more realistic for a regulated environment.

01

Payer Summary Cards

Condense diagnosis, treatment request, medication history, rationale, and required evidence into one reviewable block.

02

Risk Factors

Surface missing documentation, weak rationale, or payer-specific requirements before submission creates delay.

03

Human Review

Require physician approval before anything is finalized so the AI supports judgment instead of replacing it.

PRODUCT TRADEOFFS

The hardest decision was what not to automate

In a healthcare product, automation can create risk when it hides uncertainty. I avoided framing AuthForm as a tool that "gets approvals." Instead, the product promise became more defensible: reduce preventable delays by improving packet readiness. That distinction matters because it gives the product a measurable operational goal without claiming control over payer decisions.

Keep

AI Summaries

Useful when they convert scattered clinical details into reviewable structure.

Add

Confidence States

Needed to show whether a recommendation is strong, incomplete, or requires physician review.

Avoid

Auto-Submission

Too risky for an MVP because clinical accountability and payer rules require explicit human review.

LEARNINGS & IMPACT

The outcome was a clearer request workflow with less avoidable back-and-forth

AuthForm was built to reduce preventable prior authorization delays by improving request quality before submission. The product makes clinical evidence easier to scan, payer requirements easier to catch, and approval packets easier to prepare.

Next

The next version would add payer rules, source evidence, and denial-pattern learning

If I continued AuthForm, I would test the workflow with physicians and administrative staff, then add payer-specific rule libraries, confidence indicators, source-linked documentation, audit trails, and denial-pattern insights. The goal would be to make the system more transparent without making the physician do more administrative work.

Reflection

AuthForm sharpened how I think about responsible AI products

The product lesson was that AI is most useful when it reduces ambiguity without hiding uncertainty. AuthForm works because it does not pretend to be the decision maker. It helps clinicians see what might go wrong, prepare stronger documentation, and keep control over the final request.
