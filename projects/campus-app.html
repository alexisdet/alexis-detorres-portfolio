AdaptAI

AI-assisted accessibility app designed to help disabled users make dressing decisions with clearer outfit guidance, adaptive recommendations, and more confidence.

TimelineJune 2026
ContextHackathon
RoleProduct Manager/UX
Result1st Place

Long Story Short

I shaped an AI accessibility concept that helps disabled users decide what to wear with more confidence

AdaptAI was built around a human problem that can feel small from the outside but deeply personal in daily life: getting dressed can be overwhelming when mobility, sensory needs, dexterity, weather, occasion, or confidence all affect the decision. I focused the product around adaptive intake, outfit support, and clear recommendations so users could move from “I do not know what works for me today” to “I have an option I can trust.”

How might we help disabled users choose outfits that support comfort, accessibility, independence, and self-expression?

Accessibility Research

To reduce friction, I focused the concept around daily dressing support and adaptive decision-making

I mapped where dressing can become difficult: choosing clothing that is physically manageable, avoiding uncomfortable materials, matching the weather or occasion, and feeling confident in the final choice. AdaptAI reframes outfit selection as a guided support system that recommends practical clothing options while keeping the user's preferences and autonomy at the center.

Solution

I turned a broad accessibility problem into a focused AI dressing-support workflow

The final concept included a lightweight intake flow, a user preference profile, AI-assisted outfit recommendations, and a guided decision path. The goal was not to tell users how they should look, but to help them identify clothing that fits their body, needs, environment, and style. The experience was scoped around clarity: ask only what matters, explain why each outfit works, and make every next step feel manageable.

Process

What I would expand next

The next iteration would add user testing with disabled users across different mobility, sensory, and dexterity needs; stronger recommendation criteria for weather, occasion, and comfort; and a wardrobe input flow that is easier to maintain. I would also add confidence indicators so users understand why a recommendation was made and can quickly swap anything that does not feel right.

Impact

Reflection

What I learned

This project strengthened my ability to turn an open-ended accessibility challenge into a focused product workflow. It also gave me practice communicating AI tradeoffs clearly: the most valuable product decision was not adding more automation, but designing guidance that feels useful, explainable, respectful, and personal.
